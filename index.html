01 · Market signal

Recovery signals are ahead of the statistical record.

Reports describe partial rebuilding. The public record still lacks a recent, consistent national snapshot. Producers, farm buyers, auctions, slaughterhouses, wholesalers, and retailers also face financing, fuel, power, roads, and security frictions.

Qualitative reading · medium

Partial recovery

There are signs of rebuilding, but no recent statistical snapshot with consistent national coverage.

Price changes hands along the chain

The farm-gate-to-retail spread and intermediation make producer income less predictable.

Weather and distance set the cost base

Flooding, drought, long routes, and the border add operating risk to an extensive activity.

Reading rule. A validated producer ranking is unavailable in the public evidence used here. The trail that can be followed is capacity and formalization through the chain.

06 · Compliance

Every transfer adds a compliance step.

The activity crosses health permits, animal movement, slaughter, taxes, land, environment, labor, and imports/exports.

Institutions identified · high
01

INSAI · health authority

INSAI states a mandate covering agricultural and animal health and disease prevention. Specific vaccination schedules, certificate requirements, and current coverage require separate service-level verification.

Operational / institutional framework
02

SIGESAI · administrative gateway

SIGESAI advertises registration, movement-permit generation/printing, and authenticity checks for listed permits, protocols, and vaccination certificates. It does not by itself prove universal coverage or interoperability with a private record.

Identified operating system
03

Slaughter and food safety

The formal chain depends on authorized slaughterhouses, food controls, and sanctions against clandestine slaughter and smuggling.

Sector regulation
04

Beyond the pen

SENIAT may enter tax, customs, or succession workflows; the specific livestock procedure requires separate verification. SENCAMER and the separate INSAI certificate portal remain additional compliance lanes, not fully verified workflows in this brief.

07 · Signals to monitor

Prices and exports point to different constraints.

Market tension

The farm-gate price and retail price diverge.

A 2025 Fedenaga statement placed the producer at around US$ 2.10/kg and described a higher downstream price. The difference is a structural signal, not a universal quotation.

Exports

Exports remain an aspiration with no stable flow.

A media-reported pause in live-cattle exports in late 2025 remains unresolved; aspirations toward China and the Gulf are not presented as a stable export industry.

08 · Moolaah in Venezuela

Venezuela can give Moolaah a real operating market.

The opportunity is a sizeable livestock base, formal movement institutions, thin current data, and a plausible need for working capital. Moolaah can enter by making local records and cash flows easier to verify.

External market evidence

Large base, weak visibility

FAOSTAT reports 16.21 million cattle and 2.01 million buffalo in 2023, both marked as official observations. The 2024 values are imputed or estimated. The gap between a large productive base and a weak current national picture creates demand for better farm-level records, not just another market estimate.

Institutions already exist

INSAI is the agricultural and animal-health authority; RUNSAI handles registration/management services; SIGESAI advertises movement-permit generation and authenticity checks; and INSAI's separate Hierros y Señales service covers animal marks. Certificate workflows are separate, while SACS handles human-food sanitary controls. These systems may anchor a local data model, but a private record would need to connect to or be accepted by the relevant authority; it cannot automatically prove ownership, health status, or a right to seize animals.

Working capital has a plausible use case

Banco de Venezuela advertises an official agricultural-credit product. Its public page confirms an agricultural-credit channel and application requirements, but does not establish livestock-loan volumes, approval rates, pricing, or access for small and medium cattle producers. A 10 November 2024 El Impulso report quoting Fedenaga described lack of credit, rural insecurity, exchange-rate instability, and requests for fiscal support. That supports a market hypothesis, not a measured credit gap.

Corridors matter more than national averages

Zulia and the South of Lake Maracaibo combine cattle, dairy, and buffalo activity; Apure, Barinas, and Guárico are associated with extensive cattle; Portuguesa and Cojedes connect livestock with broader agricultural commerce; Lara has a dairy and genetics profile. These are starting hypotheses, not a validated ranking. Choose a corridor because a partner, buyer, and data trail are available.

What Moolaah can do first

Make local records financeable

Connect producer records, brands, movement permits, veterinary documents, animal counts, weights, health events, buyer deliveries, and payment history. The first output should be a usable risk file for a producer or supply-chain partner. A token is not necessary to test this value.

Where to enter

Use an anchor with cash-flow visibility

Start with a dairy collection network, slaughterhouse buyer, producer cooperative, feed or veterinary-input distributor, or established farm operator. An anchor can validate records, provide repayment data, and show where control of the animal and payment actually sits.

Market-entry sequence

1 · Choose

Select one corridor and one cash cycle using partner access, records, payment visibility, logistics, and legal clarity.

2 · Prove

Run a non-token data pilot that measures identity accuracy, data completeness, offline recovery, verification time, and cost per case.

3 · Finance

Add controlled working capital tied to an input, buyer, or documented repayment source, then track realized outcomes and recoveries.

09 · Sources and limits

Sources, estimates, and gaps.

Sources are grouped by provenance. Sector and media data stay labeled as such so estimates do not become official facts.

FAO / FAOSTAT Comparable 2023-2024 dataHigh · official 2023 / estimated 2024

Primary base: FAOSTAT · QCL and its normalized bulk archive. The 2023 headline rows are flagged A (official figure). In 2024, cattle, buffalo, cattle-meat, and buffalo-meat rows are flagged I (imputed), while raw cow's milk is flagged E (estimated). DBnomics is a mirror/discovery source; the checked API currently ends at 2022 and is not used to substantiate the 2023–2024 cards.

INSAI / SIGESAI Health, registration, mobility, and certificatesHigh · institutional portals

INSAI states the agricultural/animal-health mandate; RUNSAI describes registration/management services; INSAI movement guidance and SIGESAI cover movement-permit workflows and listed authenticity checks; Hierros y Señales is a separate animal-mark service. Import/export certificates use a separate portal whose current end-to-end workflow was not verified. SACS is the relevant food-sanitary lane for products, establishments, and transport. Availability and legal effect of each procedure may change.

OFAC Venezuela sanctionsLicense-specific compliance

OFAC's Venezuela program page contains active sanctions, general licenses, FAQs, and license-specific authorizations. An agricultural authorization is not a blanket clearance for Venezuelan livestock, meat, dairy, finance, or technology. Any proposed transaction requires counterparty-, entity-, property-, and license-specific screening.

AN · 11 Jun 2026 Reform in first readingHigh · legislative source

National Assembly note. It is a proposal in first reading: it is not equivalent to an enacted law or current regulation.

Industry associations / sector / media Estimates and signalsMedium / low · not census-based

A 3 April 2024 Finanzas Digital interview attributed to the Observatorio Nacional del Circuito Lácteo reported that 12% of milk went to industry and 88% to cheese, while also claiming 65% of production came from border states. A 17 June 2025 Banca y Negocios report attributed a 14% estimate to the same sector source. Together these indicate a 12–14% sector-estimate range, not an official series; the denominators and methodology are not published. The 80% small/medium-producer claim and 15–20% buffalo-supermarket estimate also remain sector/media estimates. Later Funvessa and government statements circulated 3–3.5 million buffalo estimates, but no published census reconciles them with FAOSTAT.

The candidate academic reference associated with the 80% claim was not independently verified and is not used as evidence. The 80% claim is reported by Banca y Negocios; the US$2.10/kg farm-gate price signal is reported by El Impulso.

USDA FAS 2024-2025 reference reportsIndustry/TDM estimates · not FAOSTAT

The 18 September 2025 report estimates 2025 beef production at 267,000 MT and cites 306,000 MT for 2024; its table source is the Venezuelan livestock and meat industry / Trade Data Monitor, explicitly “not Official USDA Data.” The 3 October 2024 report forecast 293,300 MT for 2024. These figures use industry/TDM definitions and should not be merged or averaged with FAOSTAT QCL.

Important gaps

No state ranking

The summary has no recent validated share by federal entity.

No confirmed buffalo census

No operational 2026 data
